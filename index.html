How it works

Finding each other signed · expiring · unreadable

Your identity is a keypair generated on your device — no accounts, no phone numbers. The only shared service is this site: a signed, expiring {peer → address} directory, plus a signed pointer saying who currently hosts each room. Peers verify every record themselves; the hub stores bytes it cannot forge and cannot read.

Talking direct · end-to-end · padded

When you join, the host hands you the room key — sealed for you alone — and you talk straight to everyone in the room. Every message gets its own key, frames are padded so even their lengths stay quiet, and the relay path, if your network needs one, carries only ciphertext.

When two people can't connect sealed · signed · timestamped

Hard networks happen. When direct delivery fails, sealed envelopes wait in a signed hub inbox and drain through this site — within a minute or two — so delivery no longer needs both sides online at once. The inbox carries only ciphertext; the recipient verifies every sender, and the hub still can't read, forge, or alter a single word. Delivered this way, the app marks the message ⇄ site.

Security, honestly

Not "military-grade" adjectives — a graded model of what this app actually protects, what it reveals, and what it doesn't claim to solve.

Protected

  • Message content — sealed on your device with per-message keys, padded to hide lengths
  • Room keys — delivered sealed per member; rotation re-seals under the old key
  • Universes — a ✦ Warp re-keys the room for everyone present; an old universe's key opens nothing new
  • Private chats — memory-only, vanish when both sides leave
  • Your identity — a keypair that exists only on your device

Intercepting traffic gets an attacker ciphertext, always.

Visible to your own hub (this site)

  • That a peer id exists and its current addresses — signed by the peer, expiring in 5 minutes
  • Which peer currently hosts which room — same signing, same expiry
  • An anonymous "an app is online" token every two minutes — random per session, never tied to an identity
  • When direct delivery fails: sealed mailbox envelopes' sender/recipient ids, timing, and size — ciphertext only, signatures verified by recipients, not the hub

The hub is your own infrastructure and can never read a word — but it (and anyone observing it) can learn this metadata. We say so instead of hiding it.

Out of scope — honest limits

  • A compromised device: malware on your machine can read your screen
  • Guessable words: a word room keeps out strangers who don't know the word — pick unguessable ones (the app rolls them for you)
  • Global traffic analysis: metadata-resistant routing is not implemented
  • Browser clients: the /join portal keeps your key in browser storage — weaker than the signed app's. Fine for casual rooms; use the app for serious ones

Questions friends actually ask

Do I need an account?

No. Open the room, type any name, you're in. Your identity is a key generated on your device the first time you join.

Where do my messages live?

On the members' devices, sealed. The main room's history survives restarts on your machine; private chats live in memory and vanish when both people leave. The website never sees or stores a single word.

What happens if this site disappears?

The site is only the address book and the fallback mailbox. Without it, the app can't introduce NEW peers to each other — but people already connected keep talking, and the site has no messages to lose.

Who can join Earth?

Anyone with the app — or a browser at /join. The first person online creates Earth and hosts it; the host can ✦ Warp at any moment, moving everyone present to a new universe while the word starts a fresh room for whoever comes next.

What's a word room?

Exactly what it sounds like: type a word at the door and you're in a room with everyone who types the same word. The word never leaves your device — each machine derives the same room from it, through a memory-hard function that makes guessing expensive. Roll a generated phrase for private rooms; Earth is the public one.

What is ✦ Warp?

The host's escape hatch, in the browser portal. Warp re-keys the room: everyone present moves together into a new universe, and the word immediately starts a fresh room for whoever types it next. A leaked word stops being a problem — your group is gone, and strangers still get a working room. Each universe is keyed separately; an old universe's key opens nothing new.

Someone was offline when I sent a message. Is it lost?

No — since 1.0.12 the message waits sealed on the site's fallback mailbox and arrives when they're back, usually within a minute or two. It's marked ⇄ site so the path is visible.

Do we all need the same version?

Yes — every release is its own room: people on different versions can't see each other. If a friend is on a newer build, download the latest from this page to join them; older versions keep working fine among themselves.

Can I chat without installing anything?

Yes — /join is the browser portal. Same sealed rooms, same words (Earth is the public one), no account. The portal keeps a session's messages only — the app keeps history.
